## Design Specificity Verdict

The territory chart is especially distinctive. This does not look like a generic documentation template.

The primary weakness is information architecture. “Structures,” “Atlas,” and “Structure-first trail” overlap. The landing recommends Counters, while Atlas recommends Multi-value registers (`src/components/LandingSignal.astro:13-21`, `src/pages/atlas/index.astro:42-50`). The interface has a clear visual world but does not have one clear starting path.

## Overall Impression

The site has an unusually coherent and credible visual identity. Reading typography, system-state feedback, and the observatory metaphor support difficult technical material well. The largest opportunity is to make the learning journey as decisive as the visual direction: one canonical entry, one supported next action, and one meaningful conclusion.

## What’s Working

- **A distinctive visual world:** Station marks, signal endpoints, chart stock, and square instrument surfaces express distributed-systems concepts without becoming a generic glowing network dashboard.
- **Excellent long-form typography:** Strong heading contrast, generous serif text, a stable reading measure, and responsive layouts support comprehension (`src/styles/global.css:58-137`).
- **Trustworthy interaction feedback:** Visible focus, reduced-motion support, explained disabled states, retained frames, and invariant checks make the labs feel rigorous rather than ornamental.

## Priority Issues

### **P1 — The site presents competing starting points**

**Why it matters:** A first-time reader must choose among Counters, Structures, Atlas, and Multi-value registers before understanding how those paths differ.

**Fix:** Establish one canonical beginner route. Make Structures the learning path and Atlas the reference map. Explain where the seven-sheet causal trail fits, and use the same first recommendation everywhere.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### **P1 — The lab requires recall instead of supporting recognition**

**Why it matters:** The reproduction procedure sits far above the interactive surface, especially on mobile. Readers must remember an ordered sequence while six operations appear equally valid.

**Fix:** Add an in-lab **Guided run** stepper with one current instruction, a visible completion state, and a clear switch to free exploration. Keep the prose procedure as reference.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

### **P2 — Lab controls exceed the reader’s decision budget**

**Why it matters:** Operations, partition controls, queue actions, trace navigation, inspectors, and invariants appear together. The interface exposes the model’s full complexity before the reader forms the core concept.

**Fix:** Group controls by replica and network. Hide unavailable inverse actions instead of showing both states. Move duplication and trace playback into an **Advanced exploration** disclosure.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### **P2 — The instructional peak has no strong ending**

**Why it matters:** After a substantial conceptual achievement, the lesson closes with references and a quiet next-sheet link. The experience ends administratively instead of consolidating learning.

**Fix:** Add a compact completion panel: what the reader observed, what they can now explain, one optional experiment, and one dominant next-sheet action.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable delight`

## Cognitive Load

The landing and indexes have moderate load; initial lab use has high load.

- **Failed:** single focus, chunking, lab grouping, one thing at a time, minimal choices, working-memory support.
- **Partial failures:** visual hierarchy and progressive disclosure.
- Decision points above four options include the Structures index (**7**), homepage trail (**7**), initial lab operations (**6**), trace navigation (**5**), and per-message actions as the queue grows.
- The strongest avoidable burden is the “memory bridge”: readers must retain the procedure from `src/content/sheets/dots-and-causal-context.mdx:165-180` while operating the lab.

## Emotional Journey

The landing starts with confidence, and the article moves effectively from uncertainty to causal clarity. The lab should be the emotional and instructional peak, but its simultaneous controls and readouts create the deepest valley.

Recovery and reassurance are strong through disabled reasons, retained frames, reset, status announcements, and invariant checks. The ending is flat: it does not mark the reader’s achievement or summarize the new mental model.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Maya, first-time engineer learner:** She receives conflicting start recommendations, then must remember an off-screen procedure while choosing among six lab operations.
- **Ravi, experienced reference seeker:** The territory chart scans well, but Structures and Atlas overlap semantically. There is no search or compact topic-to-sheet index.
- **Noah, keyboard/mobile learner:** Focus treatment, source order, and skip navigation are strong. Repeated glossary links add tab stops, and the long mobile lab separates controls from later results and inspectors.

## Minor Observations

- Repeated “Read now” labels add little information to the Structures index.
- Desktop index pages leave useful horizontal space unused despite the dense subject.
- Mobile article metadata repeats “Structure-first trail · 3 of 7.”
- “Source Planned” in the footer reads as unfinished product chrome.
- A persistent **Jump to lab** action would help on very long articles.
- The documented type scale does not cover many specialized diagram and lab-label sizes, which causes widespread detector noise and weakens token governance.
